## Summary

This paper introduces **SciAudit**, a large-scale benchmark for evaluating Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in the task of open-ended scientific error detection in full research papers. SciAudit is constructed with two subsets: *Gold*, containing 2,938 human-verified errors from 293 problematic papers, and *Silver*, containing 5,441 controlled, model-injected errors in 527 high-quality papers. Each error is annotated with detailed metadata (location, evidence, reasoning, type), enabling a fine-grained, quality-aware evaluation. The benchmark spans 16 scientific domains and 7 sources. The authors evaluate 13 state-of-the-art MLLMs under both “thinking” and “non-thinking” settings, analyzing performance across various capabilities and error types.

---

## Strengths

### 1. **Motivation Clarity**
- The paper is well-motivated: it clearly articulates the growing challenge in peer review due to the increased scientific output, and the need for automated tools to assist in error detection.
- The limitations of current benchmarks (scale, realism, granularity, openness) are convincingly described.

### 2. **Methodological Novelty**
- The construction of SciAudit is novel in both scale and design:
  - *Gold* subset leverages public peer reviews and rigorous human verification.
  - *Silver* subset employs a multi-model, multi-stage (Draft–Check–Refine) pipeline for error injection and quality control, reducing bias and enhancing diversity.
- The evaluation framework goes beyond binary metrics, introducing a quality-aware, multi-dimensional scoring mechanism (localization, evidence, reasoning, type).
- Errors are categorized by capability (cognition, perception, reasoning, composite), allowing nuanced capability analysis.

### 3. **Experimental Completeness**
- The authors comprehensively benchmark 13 MLLMs, including both proprietary and open-source models, across both subsets and in both “thinking” and “non-thinking” modes.
- The analysis is thorough: error-type analysis, capability analysis, ablation by complexity factors, false positive breakdown, and model-specific error re-verification.
- The benchmark’s robustness is validated through careful inter-rater agreement studies, model-agnostic scoring, and cross-generator analysis.

### 4. **Writing Fluency and Clarity**
- The paper is well-organized and clearly written, with distinct sections for methodology, dataset construction, evaluation, and analysis.
- Figures and tables are effectively used to illustrate benchmark coverage, error examples, and experimental results.
- Prompts, annotation protocols, and evaluation criteria are meticulously and transparently documented in the appendix.

---

## Weaknesses

### 1. **Methodological Concerns**
- The “Silver” subset, while thoughtfully constructed, is still based on synthetic errors generated by MLLMs; there remains a risk that such errors may not fully capture the subtlety or diversity of real-world scientific mistakes, despite high manual validation scores.
- The “thinking” vs. “non-thinking” paradigm is treated as a switch in available APIs; deeper analysis of what constitutes “thinking” in current MLLMs (e.g., explicit reasoning steps, chain-of-thought) could be strengthened.

### 2. **Experimental Scope**
- While the benchmark covers a wide range of domains, it is unclear whether some scientific fields (e.g., highly mathematical or code-heavy disciplines) are underrepresented.
- The evaluation focuses on model performance in terms of recall, precision, and quality-aware F1, but user-facing metrics (e.g., time to review, usability in practical peer review settings) are not explored.
- The “agentic” aspect (i.e., how such models could be integrated into real reviewer workflows) is left for future work.

### 3. **Writing and Clarity**
- The paper is dense in places; some details (especially in the appendix) could be summarized more succinctly in the main text for better readability.
- Some tables and figures (e.g., error-type breakdowns) could be referenced more explicitly in relevant discussion sections to aid navigation.

### 4. **Reproducibility and Availability**
- While the authors promise to release the dataset and code upon acceptance, clarity on timelines and licensing (especially given the involvement of proprietary APIs and data sources) would be beneficial.

2. **Clarify “Thinking” Mode**: Provide more operational detail or ablation on what “thinking” means for different models, and how it affects error localization and reasoning.
